Why choose it

Resala's main asset is its people. HR keeps the community alive through engagement, belonging, inclusion, culture, recognition, feedback, and accountability.

Guiding question

Can you notice when Resala's people are losing energy or feeling disconnected, then take responsibility for bringing the community back to life?

Interview task direction

Build an HR engagement plan: onboarding, check-ins, recognition, inactive-member follow-up, bonding ideas, and accountability rules.

Pick your head

This committee has 4 heads — you apply to one

Each head owns a different piece of the work. Read all of them before you decide which one is yours.

Engagement Head

The Connector

Plans and leads activities that help volunteers build friendships, stay engaged, and enjoy being a part of Resala. Organizes team-building initiatives, gathers member feedback, and works with other committees to strengthen participation and build a strong sense of community.

Inclusion Head

The Advocate

Ensures that every volunteer feels welcomed, supported, and able to participate fully in Resala. Coordinates accessibility and inclusion initiatives, supports visually impaired and international students, and works with committees to remove barriers to participation.

Tracking System Head

The Organizer

Keeps Resala's HR data accurate and usable. Owns the volunteer database and attendance tracking system, works hand-in-hand with the Tech Team to keep it running, and turns raw data into reports that make the club's decisions, like VOM and COM, actually fair.

Recognition & Accountability Head

The Fair Judge

Makes sure effort gets seen and gaps get addressed — fairly, every time. Reviews attendance and performance data, follows up with members who've gone quiet, oversees warnings and demotions when they're truly needed, and coordinates recognition (VOM/COM) with the Branding Team.

Role requirements

Read this as a checklist

Leadership requirement

Lead by building a community system. Make people feel seen while keeping expectations and accountability clear.

Ownership requirement

Notice disconnection early. Follow up when energy drops and protect the culture before it weakens.

Skills requirement

  • People skills.
  • Empathy and emotional intelligence.
  • Communication.
  • Inclusion and community-building.
  • Ability to handle sensitive situations.
  • Follow-up.
  • Ability to build people systems.
  • Balance between kindness and accountability.

Preferred experiences or passions

  • You care about people, culture, belonging, and community.
  • You naturally notice when someone is disconnected.
  • You can be kind without avoiding accountability.
